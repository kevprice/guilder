# The house that could not say goodbye

Current implemented narrative direction. Spoilers throughout.

Full authored material: [Companion lives and farewells](HOMECOMINGS.md) and [Room diaries and testimony](PERSONAL_RECORDS.md). Regenerate these references with `node write-homecoming-reference.mjs` after editing `homecoming.js`.

The central question is: **When does saving something become refusing to let it live?**

The house began by overcoming distance. Its first doors brought medicine, shelter and reunions. The guild gradually treated the people and places it connected as possessions. Vey’s understandable desire to put nobody beyond help became a willingness to override refusal.

## Rowan and Halden

Rowan joined because they had no secure home. Halden gave them a room and a place at a table. They planned a journey without a contract; Rowan repeatedly postponed it. Their intimacy is established without assigning it an exclusively romantic or familial label.

Rowan helped catalogue acquired rooms. Their diaries admit renaming a family kitchen and leaving its owner’s protest out of the catalogue. Other witnesses contradict the official account. Rowan’s complicity is ordinary and consequential, rather than a hidden explanation for every tragedy.

Halden’s emergency binding saved Rowan after the stair collapse. Following the severance, Halden searched for a treatment outside, raised Mara, treated patients and had an ordinary life. Letters span twenty-nine years. Halden died before Rowan became reachable. Mara inherits the token and chooses to bring it back, while retaining responsibilities elsewhere.

The infirmary revelation establishes these facts even if optional diary pages have not been collected. The tower contains further letters. Release gives Rowan a future without pretending to recover the thirty years they lost.

## Personal records

`homecoming.js` contains ninety readable records: room pages and companion correspondence. Every main, regional and branching dungeon room has a record; the entrance, quarters, cellar, tower and labyrinth also have pages. Restoring a room, reaching a floor or completing its investigation makes its record accessible. Records have labelled map interactions and a dedicated Diaries page with a room selector. Each can be recovered once and remains available individually. Collected text also enters Journal.

Rowan’s account is not authoritative. The kitchen owner, burial keeper, apprentice, prisoner and workers provide conflicting evidence. There is no random diary generation. Optional evidence adds context rather than blocking the main liberation route.

## Companions and departures

All thirty recruitable companions have a personal homecoming record, destination and permanent farewell. Initial companions require their existing personal quests. Awakened residents require the infirmary truth. Regional companions require completion of their regional campaign. Read their correspondence, then speak with them while they are at home. The player can close the conversation and return later.

Departure is explicit, permanent and voluntary at the decision point. The companion remains in the register with memories and a farewell account, leaves the map and expedition pool, and returns equipment to shared storage. Controlling a departing resident returns control to Rowan. Away, injured and dead residents cannot be released through this conversation. Saves preserve departures.

Living companions return to communities or choose new work. Preserved companions confront lives that continued while they were absent. Lark, Ves, Fenn, Sorrel, Sal and Tavi are spirits; their personal evidence establishes their deaths, and their farewells let them pass on. Other recruits remain living or preserved. There is no blanket revelation that everyone is dead.

The implemented regional farewells are concluding correspondence and scenes after the existing twelve-job campaign. They do not yet add separate playable family-reunion locations. Postponement is supported; escalating resentment, negotiated departure deadlines and off-site ally benefits are not yet simulated.

## The Guest

The Guest learns voices and habits from traces in connected rooms. Its aim is to draw the network into one enduring interior where separation becomes impossible. At the threshold it offers a dinner with Halden, the dead and those who left. A departed person now insists they always wanted to stay; Halden repeats an old conversation instead of acknowledging the years outside. Rowan closes the shutter.

Both replacement systems and both house endings remain valid. Rowan is freed in every completed main-story ending. The Guest loses this road; the game does not claim its universal destruction.

## Editorial work still needed

The new correspondence gives every recruit personal stakes, but most regional jobs still follow their previous rescue-and-repair structure. Longer relationship scenes, disagreements between companions, reactive conversations about necromancy, playable reunion destinations and the physical contraction of returned rooms remain future work. These should extend this canon rather than silently treating short farewell scenes as complete personal campaigns.
