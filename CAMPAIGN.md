# The Bound House — narrative bible and branching campaign

> Current narrative revision: [STORY_REVISION.md](STORY_REVISION.md) supersedes older character and Guest assumptions below. Rowan and Halden planned a life beyond contracts. Halden raised Mara during the years outside and died before the entrance reopened. Personal records expose Rowan’s complicity in room acquisitions. Every companion has a homecoming, including six spirits who can pass on. The Guest offers preservation without the freedom to leave.

This file contains author knowledge and ending spoilers. The game exposes each fact through its own discovery or conversation. It is a working authored campaign, intended for revision before the later mechanics and visual pass.

## The dramatic question

Can a guild built to bring impossible things home learn when to leave them where they belong? Can Rowan rebuild a home without accepting captivity as the price of having survived?

Gold remains practical pressure: every revelation brings a room that needs workers and materials, a person who needs paying, or a journey that removes somebody useful from the house. The mystery comes from specific decisions and witnesses, rather than a sequence of interchangeable monsters. The house is a collection of relationships made physical.

The new guild inherits a complicated institution. Its rescuers saved real people. Its collectors also treated rooms, ecosystems and communities as acquisitions. Public gratitude and public anger can both be justified. A returning room should leave the player asking who used it before, who lost it, and what consent means now.

## Private chronology

Thirty years ago, the guild already used small anchors to connect distant rooms. A shore landing, a ship fragment, an archive and a greenhouse had been drawn into the estate. Some removals were negotiated, some obscured by debt and authority. Junior members, including Rowan, understood little of the machinery or its costs.

Professor Ada Merrow helped calculate a permanent joining to a supposedly empty destination. Its silence was interpreted as absence, although an archive warning suggested it might be listening. Commander Vey authorised the joining. A dangerous intelligence began crossing, imitating voices associated with the guild. It is called the guest in surviving records; it has no confirmed public name or cosmology yet.

Lookout Elian rang an alarm first to signal the joining, then to signal its severance. The contradictory orders became a public story in which he appeared responsible for the loss. Surgeon Halden found Rowan mortally injured while helping people on the stair. He bound their life to the house, simultaneously saving them and making their mind the stabilising anchor. The survivors cut the road, stranding different rooms at their remote destinations. The guest did not complete the crossing.

Halden left no mandate that Rowan must stay forever. His last recorded instruction rejects turning an emergency into their obligation. The remaining leaders dispersed. Rowan was isolated because the entrance road could not be reliably found until Mara returned the token. This explains the opening without making Rowan passive, foolish, or unable to act as a leader.

The intelligence is dangerous but not the explanation for every human failure. It did not make the guild steal an orchard, dismiss witnesses or hide a cost. Denying it a road is the final technical problem; deciding what the new guild should become is the continuing human problem.

## Campaign structure

| Chapter | Player work | Discovery | Lasting question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · A wage and a wall | Recruit, earn, train Strength, breach and restore | A concealed ledger and a voluntarily returned small anchor | What did the guild bring home, and who wanted it back? |
| II · Things brought home | Follow Sera’s coast; recover the archive | A ship was someone’s home; a chair embodies an unfulfilled promise | Rescue people now or preserve knowledge that may help others later? |
| III · The price of wonders | Restore archive, greenhouse and observatory; pursue personal leads | Corrected accounts, Alder’s stolen seasons, Elian’s contradictory alarms | Is the house a refuge, a research institution, or something governed by its neighbours? |
| IV · Who closed the door | Retrieve the failing infirmary’s memory cylinder | Rowan’s injury, Halden’s binding, deliberate severance | Who gets to hear a survivor’s private history? |
| V · A different kind of guild | Prepare a covenant or an independently closable lattice | A safe road can survive refusal without a captive anchor | Who can withdraw consent or stop an unsafe joining? |
| VI · The road beyond | Stabilise the chamber, remove the binding, walk out as Rowan | Freedom is an action rather than another reward screen | What does Rowan choose to return to? |

There are sixteen contracts in the catalogue: ten authored story/personal/preparation contracts and six repeatable income contracts. A playthrough uses one of the two final preparation contracts. Chapters mark discoveries, not elapsed-day timers. Physical exploration never advances expeditions.

## The house and what each return means

**Entrance and quarters.** A survivor’s last connected world becomes a working guild. The brass token makes visits possible. Resident conversations take place here; people acknowledge work and discoveries while retaining their own histories. Rowan’s original boundary ends before the service room.

**Service room.** Its records were concealed behind a wall bricked from inside. Strength 10 opens the physical barrier, but workers and materials still cost 70 gold. The hidden ledger points to Wren’s Crossing and warns against the great threshold. Breach, restoration and discovery remain different persistent states.

**Flooded cellar.** The ship’s bow was transported with possessions still in it. It is evidence that a guild curiosity was another person’s ordinary home. Repaired stairs reach a dry landing for every adventurer; Water breathing is required to reach the submerged drain wheel beyond it. Opening the sluice permanently exposes the cellar floor and lets every resident enter. Captain Sera’s name and chart connect the discovery to the navigator expedition. The main campaign also reaches Sera through Rowan’s landing inscription, avoiding a mandatory underwater detour.

**Returned landing.** It comes back from Wren’s Crossing with a chair for people the guild promised to bring home. Rowan gains inscription-reading and a longer boundary. The player chooses a visitors’ room or a memorial and study. Welcoming gains community ties; remembering gains knowledge. The surviving oath makes the distinction about purpose, not good-versus-evil furniture.

**Archive.** Sera’s seal reconnects the room. A magically literate resident opens and restores it. The official history and witness corrections survive together. Open records protect vulnerable names and admit public corrections; a research archive verifies accounts before publication. Both preserve originals and reveal the orchard, lens and warning about the listening destination. Their methods affect trust and research readiness.

**Greenhouse.** Alder lost ordinary seasons when its ecosystem was taken. Edda will give the room’s anchor but asks who keeps the only viable root bank. Returning seeds leaves the village a future and permits a hospice in the reclaimed room. Taking the roots with payment and a written promise permits a botanical laboratory. The hospice reduces future injuries by one day; the laboratory discounts final containment preparation. Neither can be selected after choosing the incompatible root outcome. The promise to share cuttings is acknowledged later.

**Observatory.** Elian’s lens reconnects the room, but his old watchtower cannot be casually abandoned. The party can bring him home to testify or leave him an ordinary replacement beacon and carry his signed record. A listening station asks who occupies each fragment; a survey station measures whether a connection is safe. Both give Rowan anchor sight and locate the failing infirmary. The method changes community or research readiness, and the infirmary letter refers back to Elian’s outcome.

**Threshold chamber.** It becomes reachable after the infirmary’s truth, but cannot be stabilised before a safe preparation. Its 220-gold cost represents materials and stabilisation rather than an arbitrary price for Rowan’s freedom. The final choice preserves a home with visiting roads or begins returning borrowed rooms by agreed dates. In either case, Rowan is unbound now. Finishing the campaign requires the player to walk left beyond the entrance boundary.

## The branching spine

| Decision | Option A | Option B | What carries forward |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wren’s bell | Accept the keeper’s warning | Listen without opening | Community trust versus a voice clue, knowledge and extra gold; both recover the small anchor |
| Landing chair | Welcome visitors | Preserve the oath | Room identity, community versus knowledge, resident trust |
| Sera’s storm | Rescue deckhands | Preserve the complete chart | Escort work and Sera’s trust versus survey knowledge and money; the archive seal always returns |
| Archive policy | Open corrected records | Verify with researchers | Different public relationship and expedition callbacks; both reveal the same essential leads |
| Alder’s roots | Return the seed bank | Study the lattice at home | Village ally and hospice versus laboratory, research and a recorded restitution promise |
| Elian’s light | Bring the witness home | Bring the signed record and instruments | Listening partner versus calibrated research; both preserve his account |
| Observatory purpose | Ask who is there | Measure safe connections | Community ties versus knowledge; both grant anchor sight |
| Infirmary record | Rowan authorises full publication | Publish the collapse, protect Rowan’s injury | Public accountability versus a limited personal-history circle; danger and essential truth are not concealed |
| Safe covenant | Enforceable guild charter | Shared community commons | Different authority in a consenting connected house |
| Safe lattice | Rotating resident keyholders | Independent outside keepers | Different oversight; no solitary brilliant custodian |
| House’s future | A home with visiting roads | Begin returning borrowed rooms | Different closing intention; both give Rowan immediate freedom |

Community ties and containment knowledge record two kinds of readiness, not a morality score. The covenant requires three community ties and costs 90 gold. The lattice always remains available after the truth and observatory, costing 140 gold, or 100 with accumulated knowledge, a laboratory or Ivo’s completed story. No personal quest or exceptionally warm relationship is required to finish. The methods are mutually exclusive once one is underway/completed.

Branch callbacks are authored: Sera reacts to the keeper/voice decision; Edda reacts to the archive policy; Elian reacts to Sera’s expedition; the infirmary account refers to Elian’s testimony; preparation acknowledges the greenhouse and Ada. These are explicit functions over saved flags, not generated prose.

## Residents over time

**Bryn: strength as responsibility rather than inheritance.** Initially she wants to restore the guild’s reputation through honest paid work. Her shield still has a door latch because her father taught her that doors should open when either side needs them. The service-room mason mark makes the house personal. In her expedition, recovering her father’s memorial conflicts with an immediate rescue. Saving the apprentice grants Steadfast and Endurance; recovering the intact names grants Keeper of names and Strength, while another crew saves the apprentice later with a lasting injury. Both stories preserve the names and acknowledge a cost. Bryn’s later conversation separates honouring the dead from being obligated to finish their lives. After the infirmary truth, she rejects Rowan’s survival becoming a duty to remain.

**Nell: a guide becoming answerable for her maps.** Initially she knows routes and distrusts claims disguised as exploration. Her grandmother’s trail was a refugee route, not a picturesque path. Nell’s personal expedition can publish a route with shelter keepers while protecting their addresses, or retain a quiet passage she helps govern. The first makes her a Wayfinder and opens escort work; the second makes her a Keeper of paths and opens careful salvage. Neither abandons refugees. The choice concerns access and control. She begins signing maps so people know whom to question. A late conversation lets Rowan invite her to walk beside them, or ask for the first steps alone. The ending remembers that invitation.

**Ivo: learning to ask rather than borrow certainty.** Initially he admires Ada and treats the binding as a research opportunity. Rowan can set experimental consent or defer personal study. Ada’s dismissive annotation challenges his belief in a teacher who has already corrected every mistake. His expedition finds her maintaining a seal by choice. The party can install a tested replacement and invite her home, or support her decision to remain with a replacement keeper and supplies. Ivo becomes a Questioner or Independent scholar. Essential knowledge returns in both cases. His later proof explicitly excludes a suffering person from its assumptions and invites outside criticism. After the truth, he distinguishes something clever from something acceptable.

Each resident has completed expedition count, trust, traits and dated shared memories. Twelve conversations unlock from participation and specific discoveries. Earlier questions retire when later events have already answered them. Replies occur once, are saved, and affect trust or readiness. At home, a speech marker and E can start the next conversation; the Guild and Stories panels provide the same conversation access. An away or injured resident cannot conduct it.

## Expedition escalation

Early escorts take one day and use starting Endurance or Agility. The quarry takes two and tests stronger arms or Guard. The navigator, orchard and lens journeys take three and introduce traversal, tracking, literacy or stats around 8–11. The infirmary and preparations take four and expect Endurance/Intellect around 11–12 or a specialist capability. Injuries become three or four days rather than two; a hospice mitigates their length. Each working day now rolls the adjusted threat independently. A medical kit treats one harm event; subsequent hits add recovery time. The advertised duration assumes the summed party stat meets the target, and weaker parties work proportionally longer.

Later repeatable work reflects discoveries: letters across Sera’s marsh, guests on a shelter route, discreet salvage through Nell’s quiet passage, and observatory fragment surveys. All are tied to a place, resident or consequence already encountered. The lantern road and doorstep mending remain available so repairs, hiring and treatment cannot permanently strand the campaign.

## Ending discipline and next writing pass

The final preparation denies the intelligence a road through this house; it does not purport to kill every dangerous thing in the world. Both safe methods have refusal and independent shutoff built into them. Rowan’s removal from the machinery precedes the final future-of-house choice, so freedom is not held hostage to a moral answer.

The final scene depends on the house’s future, companionship and Nell’s explicit invitation. Other recruited companions with developed trust wait without demanding an oath to return. The closing playable action gives weight to the boundary the player has encountered all campaign long.

This is now a complete, playable narrative backbone, not a claim that a finished long-form RPG exists. The next narrative pass can add room-specific ambient exchanges, letters received several days after decisions, deeper disagreement between residents, Vey and Halden as distinct surviving relationships, alternate events inside multi-stage expeditions, and follow-through on restitution. The next user-directed phase remains mechanics, art/style and UI; this pass keeps the existing visual language and single-decision expedition model while supplying the campaign it can carry.

## Narrative revision: evidence and character voice

The player-facing Stories screen now reports discovered evidence and immediate actions. The abstract house-purpose paragraph and early community/research counters have been removed from it. Scores remain mechanical preparation resources, explained in Contracts when the preparation plans become relevant. Summaries never treat expedition count alone as evidence of a personal discovery.

The resident conversations have been rewritten. Bryn speaks as a working mason who needs wages and wants news of her father. Nell checks bridges, returns and shelter routes. Ivo brings correspondence and calculations from his missing teacher. They discuss specific work and evidence rather than delivering a shared set of maxims. After the infirmary revelation their reactions remain practical and distinct.

The three optional survivors give partial, complementary testimony. Oren braced the cellar using stone-skin magic; a bent casting ring kept him petrified. Stair repair workers shore the beam before that ring is removed. Maelin received Vey’s three-stroke evacuation order, then heard a false fourth stroke from the tower alarm. Her frost ward trapped the bell, rope and herself until its repeating rune could be disabled. Elian was the outside lookout; his watchtower signals and Maelin’s internal evacuation bell are separate instruments. Ysra linked a live map to the house to guide evacuees toward the infirmary. When that exit vanished, an unfinished tracking rune pulled her into its ink. Completing/cancelling that rune releases her.

Their accounts do not reveal who Halden carried. The infirmary recording remains the source of Rowan’s injury and binding. The mechanical awakening gates, optional status, save IDs, stats and abilities are unchanged. Their personal replies now lead to repair surveys, a safe alarm, marked return routes or written witness accounts. Old generic conversation outcomes are replaced in the current Stories recap by the authored text for the saved choice.


The lower labyrinth’s warm east door reveals a dragon nesting around the old furnace. This optional internal threat uses STR 60 and ten base days, with 85% base daily threat reduced by the party’s defence and relevant abilities. Clearing progress is permanent. Residents can withdraw immediately, recover and return; the hoard remains unknown until the dragon is killed. It contains 240 gold and the missing forge tools. It does not gate Rowan’s main story.


## After Rowan’s freedom: the eight roads

The personal binding mystery ends at the entrance gate. The guild’s responsibilities continue. The first letter from Tamsin starts the Lantern Fen campaign; each completed regional settlement introduces the next. These are specific repair and rescue efforts with twelve jobs each, written reports and named keepers, rather than unexplained magical connections. The sixth job returns a labelled key and four rooms. Regional recruits become available after the third job. The last four jobs are marked lethal; optional hunts appear after the eighth and carry higher rewards.

| Region | Keeper | Problem and resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lantern Fen | Tamsin | A reversed drainage pump floods inhabited houses. Rescue the school, recover its plans and impeller, then reverse and test the outflow. |
| Ash March | Kest | A furnace still obeys an evacuation order. Close fuel branches, recover the last order and cool the engine before sealing its trench. |
| Glass Coast | Corin | A duplicate lighthouse lens creates false distress lights. Compare registers, rescue the watch and remove the copied beacon. |
| Rootwild | Asha | A shelter-tree spell has trapped its village. Pause growth before unwinding roots; remove the parasite and return local control. |
| White Stair | Neris | A broken weather ward repeats snowfall. Recover the measurement cylinder and counterweight, then inspect the thaw route. |
| Clockwork Ward | Perrin | Guards enforce a thirty-year-old emergency charter. Preserve the arrest records, release prisoners and advance the court clock. |
| Undersea Vault | Liora | The treasury intake draws seawater through town wells. Rescue divers, preserve the ownership register and seal the intake. |
| Fallen Star | Sable | A damaged beacon summons predators through connected roads. Count travellers, close unsafe roads and replace the transmitter with manual lamps. |

Reports may be filed with local keepers for community trust or studied for containment knowledge; both leave the settlement a copy. Each successful job records its concrete result. All plot reports and further room restoration pause while home rooms are damaged. Completed reports disappear from active Contracts and remain in Journal.
